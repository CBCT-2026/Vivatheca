Keratocyst in the lower jaw (odontogenic keratocyst)

This 3D model follows one real, published case. A man aged 54 had a keratocyst at the root tips of two lower left teeth (34 and 35). A keratocyst is a sac in the jaw, filled with thick fluid. It is not cancer, but it often comes back. The surgeon took out the sac, shaved the bone wall and kept the teeth.1

    What is removed. The surgeon opens a small window in the bone between 34 and 35, lifts out the whole sac and shaves about 1.5 mm off the bone wall. The tips of the two roots are cut off (3 mm each). The teeth had root canal treatment first, so they can stay.

    The space left. The hollow in the bone is about the size of a large grape. The teeth stay in place.

    Healing. The space is filled with a gel made from the patient’s own blood (PRF). New bone fills the space over months. On X-rays, it had healed by 3 years.

    The real case

    A man aged 54 had a dull ache in his lower left jaw near the small back teeth. The gum between 34 and 35 was a little swollen. A 3D X-ray showed a hollow of 1.5 × 1.7 × 0.9 cm close to the nerve for the lip. Fluid taken with a needle was thick and yellow, and tests showed a keratocyst.1

    He chose to keep his teeth. 34 and 35 had root canal treatment first. Then the surgeon took out the cyst through a window in the bone, cut off the root tips and shaved the bone wall with an ultrasonic tool. The space was filled with PRF.1

    He had no numb lip. Three years later the bone had healed and the cyst had not come back. Keratocysts can come back late, so checks go on for at least 10 years.1

    Ways to treat a keratocyst

    There is no single best way. Surgeons choose by the size of the cyst, how close it is to nerves and teeth, whether it has come back before and what the patient prefers.12 The diagram shows the jaw cut across, with a tooth on top and the cyst in yellow.

    1. 1 Removing the sac (enucleation). The whole sac is lifted out in one piece. The hollow fills with new bone over months.
    2. 2 Opening the cyst into the mouth (marsupialisation). A window is cut and the sac lining is stitched to the mouth lining. The cyst drains and slowly shrinks. Used for big cysts.
    3. 3 Draining with a small tube (decompression). A tube keeps the cyst open so it shrinks. Months later, the smaller cyst is taken out.
    4. 4 Shaving the bone wall (peripheral ostectomy). After the sac is out, a thin layer of the bone wall (1 to 2 mm) is shaved off with a drill or an ultrasonic tool. This was done in this case.
    5. 5 Carnoy’s solution. A strong liquid is put on the bone wall for a few minutes to kill cells left behind. A newer form (modified Carnoy’s) leaves out one of its chemicals.
    6. 6 Freezing (cryotherapy). The bone wall is frozen with liquid nitrogen. This kills cells about 1.5 mm deep. The bone is weaker for a few weeks.
    7. 7 A cream called 5-FU. A cream that is also used for some skin cancers is put into the hollow on a gauze strip, then taken out.
    8. 8 Removing part of the jaw (resection). The piece of jaw that holds the cyst is removed. It is kept for very large cysts, or ones that keep coming back, because it is a much bigger operation.

    How often does it come back? In large reviews, it came back in about 1 in 3 people after opening the cyst alone, and in about 1 in 5 after removing the sac alone. Treating the bone wall as well lowered this. Removing part of the jaw had the lowest rate, but it is a much bigger operation.23

    For clinicians: how this model was built

    • Case: 54-year-old man; unilocular radiolucency at the roots of 34 and 35, 1.5 × 1.7 × 0.9 cm on CBCT, close to the mental nerve, with a crestal cortical perforation between 34 and 35. Aspiration: thick keratinous fluid. Pre-operative endodontics on 34 and 35; enucleation through an enlarged crestal window, apicectomy of 34 and 35, piezo peripheral ostectomy, PRF; teeth kept. No recurrence or paraesthesia at 36 months.1
    • Model: cyst 17 × 9 × 13 mm, centred between the apices of 34 and 35 and kept 4.6 mm above the lower border. Cavity after 1.5 mm peripheral ostectomy; crestal window 4.4 × 5.6 mm between 34 and 35. Apicectomy 3 mm on each root. Bone removed 1.8 cm³. The mesial root of 36 touches the cavity edge in the model.
    • Our choices: which dimension is vertical is not stated; we used 1.7 cm mesiodistal, 1.3 cm vertical and 0.9 cm buccolingual so the cyst fits the jaw height. Root canal fillings are not modelled.
