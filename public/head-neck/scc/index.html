Mouth cancer in the lower jaw (squamous cell carcinoma)

This 3D model follows one real, published case. A woman aged 72 had a cancer on the gum of her right lower jaw, and it had grown into the bone. Surgeons took out the right side of her lower jaw and rebuilt it with bone from her leg.1

    Before surgery. The cancer (dark red) sits on the gum beside the right back teeth and has grown into the bone. A second, small patch sits on the inner side of the gum near the right front teeth.

    What is removed. The right side of the lower jaw, from the jaw joint to the front teeth, comes out in one piece with its 8 teeth. The front cut is about 1 cm from the cancer.

    The gap. Without its right side, the jaw cannot keep its shape. The chin would move to the right, and eating and speaking would be hard.

    Rebuilding the jaw. A piece of bone from the lower leg (fibula) is cut into three parts and shaped like the missing jaw. Small titanium plates hold the parts. Its blood vessels are joined to vessels in the neck, so the bone stays alive.

    The real case

    In 2019 a woman aged 72 saw a surgeon about white patches on her gums. Over 18 months the patches spread, and part of them turned into cancer. In 2020 the gum beside her right back teeth was swollen. A small piece of tissue (biopsy) showed cancer, and a scan showed it had grown into the jaw bone.1

    The team took out the right side of her lower jaw. They rebuilt it with bone from her left leg, and she had radiotherapy after surgery. Nine months later the new bone had taken well, and she could drink by mouth.1

    Two years later a new cancer grew on the left side of her gum, and she needed another operation. This is why people who have had mouth cancer need regular checks for many years.1

    How the jaw is rebuilt

    When part of the jaw bone is removed, it is best replaced with living bone. Bone from the lower leg with its own blood supply (a fibula free flap) is the most common choice.23 The fibula is the thin bone on the outer side of the lower leg. People can walk without its middle part, because the big leg bone (tibia) carries the weight.2

    When the jaw bone stays and only soft tissue is removed (gum, cheek lining, floor of the mouth or tongue), a thin flap of skin from the forearm (radial forearm free flap) is often used instead.45

    Surgeons fix bone with plates and screws in the same few steps each time. Diagram 3 shows them. See the steps in 3D.11

    For clinicians: how this model was built

    • Case: gingival proliferative verrucous leukoplakia with malignant transformation. SCC of the right buccal gingiva 45–47 with a second focus on the lingual gingiva 42–43; PET showed mandibular invasion; right submandibular node. Right segmental mandibulectomy, left fibula free flap, adjuvant radiotherapy.1
    • Extent is taken from the one-month OPG (Image 2A), because the article gives no operative detail. The fibula runs from the right condylar region to the left incisor region in three segments: ramus, body and parasymphysis. There is no fixation at the upper end, which suggests disarticulation with the condyle removed; we modelled it that way. Brown class IIc.13
    • Model: cut between 31 and 32; teeth removed 41–47 and 31. Specimen 21.7 cm³; 106 mm from condyle to the anterior cut. Fibula in three segments (ramus 39 mm, body 62 mm, parasymphysis 30 mm, measured along the segment axes) joined by closing-wedge osteotomies: at each bend (54° at the angle, 40° at the parasymphysis) the cut faces meet on the plane that bisects the bend, the way the fibula is shaped in the standard technique and in open-source virtual planning software; the model keeps a 0.6 mm gap so the cuts stay visible.26 Miniplates as on the OPG: two at the native bone junction, one between the anterior and body segments, an L-plate at the angle (24 screws; the two overlapping corner holes of the old L-plate are now one). Plates and screws follow the Vivatheca fixation standard (how plates and screws are placed): 2.0 mm miniplates, 1.0 mm thick, with countersunk holes and monocortical 2.0 mm screws along the bone normal, at least two screws on each side of every junction.78 Miniplate and reconstruction-plate fixation of fibula flaps are both in use; a meta-analysis found more plate-related complications with miniplates, while other series report fewer plate exposures with miniplates, so the evidence is mixed.910
    • Our choices: tumour 24 × 14 × 18 mm at 45–47 and 10 × 6 × 8 mm at 42–43 (sizes not published). The model leaves 8.5 mm of bone between the 42–43 focus and the anterior cut. The case used a left fibula; the model uses a right fibula mesh.
