Ameloblastoma in the lower jaw

This 3D model follows one real, published case. A man aged 32 had an ameloblastoma in the front of his left lower jaw. It is a growth that is not cancer, but it keeps growing into bone. Surgeons took out the top part of the jaw with four teeth and kept the lower edge. A metal plate keeps the thin edge strong.1

    Making it strong. A titanium plate with 9 holes runs along the lower edge of the jaw, held by 6 screws. Later, the missing teeth can be replaced.

    The real case

    A man aged 32 had a slow swelling in the front of his left lower jaw. An X-ray showed a dark patch made of bubbles between the left canine and the first small back tooth. A 3D X-ray showed that the outer wall of the jaw was pushed out. A biopsy showed ameloblastoma.1

    The surgeons worked through the mouth and through a cut under the jaw. They took out the growth with four teeth and the top of the jaw, and kept the lower edge. Only about 6 mm of height was left, so they added a plate. Three months later he was healing well.1

    Ameloblastoma can come back if some is left behind. It comes back more often after smaller operations, so people need X-ray checks for many years.2

    How the jaw is kept strong

    When the lower edge of the jaw can be kept, the surgeon removes only the top part (a rim resection). If the edge left behind is thin, a titanium plate along it stops it breaking.1

    If the growth had reached the lower edge, the full height of the jaw would be removed. The authors planned bone from the hip for a gap shorter than 5 cm, or a fibula from the leg for a longer gap.1 See the mouth cancer page for a diagram of the fibula flap.

    For clinicians: how this model was built

    • Case: 32-year-old man; multilocular radiolucency between 33 and 34, clinically 32–35 (about 2 × 3 cm) with buccal expansion; plexiform-follicular ameloblastoma. Marginal (rim) mandibulectomy 32–35 by combined intraoral and submandibular approach. About 6 mm residual height, reinforced with a 2.0 mm, 9-hole titanium reconstruction plate.1
    • Context: for solid/multicystic ameloblastoma, recurrence is about 3 times higher after conservative treatment than after resection.2 Marginal resection keeps continuity when an adequate inferior border can be preserved; the article does not report margin widths.
    • Model: vertical cuts at the 31/32 and 35/36 contacts; horizontal floor leaving 6.0–8.4 mm of bone across 32–35 (6.0 mm at 35). Specimen 2.8 cm³. Plate 58 mm, 9 holes at 6.5 mm pitch, 3.2 mm above the lower border; screws in the three end holes on each side.
    • Our choices: the growth is four lobules, 15 × 19 × 16 mm in total, kept 2 mm above the floor of the cut. This is smaller than the clinical 2 × 3 cm swelling because the swelling includes the expanded cortex. Screw positions are not published.
