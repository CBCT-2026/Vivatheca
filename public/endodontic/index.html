What the model shows

These models show a back tooth from the lower jaw, in its bone. One tooth is healthy. The other has a dead nerve and an infection at the tips of its roots. Tap Cut open to see inside the tooth and the bone.

In the middle of each tooth is the pulp. This is the nerve and blood vessels. It runs down inside each root in thin canals.

The pink layer is the gum. It covers the bone and the necks of the teeth. Tap Take off the gum to see the bone and roots under it.17

We drew the canals and the drainage channel wider than real ones, so you can see them.

How a tooth abscess forms

Deep decay can reach the pulp. Germs get in, and the pulp can die. Then the germs spread out of the tips of the roots, into the bone.1

The body fights the germs. The bone around the root tips wears away. On an X-ray this shows as a dark area.17

Pus can find a way out through a thin channel to the gum. You may see a small pimple on the gum. This kind of abscess often hurts only a little, or not at all.1

A tooth with a dead pulp cannot heal on its own. The germs stay inside the tooth.1

How dentists treat it

There are two main choices. Root canal treatment saves the tooth. Or the dentist can take the tooth out.1

In root canal treatment, the dentist cleans the germs out of the pulp space and the canals. Then they fill and seal the canals. A back tooth often needs a crown after this, to protect it.18

After treatment, the bone at the root tips can grow back. This takes time. Your dentist may check it on an X-ray after a year, and for up to four years.8

Teeth that already have a dark area at the root tip heal a little less often than teeth without one. A well-sealed filling or crown on top helps.9

治療(ちりょう)の方法(ほうほう)

主(おも)な選択肢(せんたくし)は2つです。根管治療(こんかんちりょう)で歯(は)を残(のこ)すか、歯(は)を抜(ぬ)くかです。1

根管治療(こんかんちりょう)では、歯科医師(しかいし)が歯髄(しずい)のあった場所(ばしょ)と根管(こんかん)から細菌(さいきん)を取(と)り除(のぞ)き、根管(こんかん)を詰(つ)めてふさぎます。奥歯(おくば)はそのあと、歯(は)を守(まも)るためにクラウンをかぶせることが多(おお)いです。18

When to see your dentist

See your dentist if you have:

  • a pimple on the gum that comes and goes
  • a tooth that hurts when you bite
  • swelling near a tooth.

Pain that starts fast with swelling can be a fast-spreading abscess. See a dentist soon.1
