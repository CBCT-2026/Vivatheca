Palatally displaced maxillary canine: surgical exposure with orthodontic alignment, or surgical removal口蓋側(こうがいそく)に転位(てんい)した上顎(じょうがく)犬歯(けんし)：開窓(かいそう)・牽引(けんいん)か、抜歯(ばっし)か

Terms:用語(ようご)： Husain J, Burden D, McSherry P, Morris D, Allen M. National clinical guidelines for management of the palatally ectopic maxillary canine. Br Dent J. 2012.

This page is for an adult upper canine (eye tooth) that has grown towards the palate, the roof of the mouth, and cannot come through by itself. The baby canine often stays in place above it.1 Left alone, the stuck canine can wear away the roots of the front teeth next to it.2

Once the tooth is clearly stuck, there are two main ways to deal with it. One is to open it up in a small surgery and pull it slowly into place with braces. The other is to take it out and decide what to do with the gap.1 Before age 13, taking out the baby canine on its own sometimes lets the adult canine correct its path.3 Here you can see the two main options side by side, with a 3D model for each. Use them to talk it through with your braces specialist. This page does not choose for you.

歯(は)が止(と)まっているとはっきりしたら、おもな方法(ほうほう)は2つです。1つは小(ちい)さな手術(しゅじゅつ)で歯(は)を出(だ)し、矯正(きょうせい)装置(そうち)でゆっくり正(ただ)しい場所(ばしょ)へ引(ひ)っぱる方法(ほうほう)。もう1つは歯(は)を抜(ぬ)いて、すき間(ま)をどうするか決(き)める方法(ほうほう)です。113歳(さい)より前(まえ)なら、乳歯(にゅうし)の犬歯(けんし)を抜(ぬ)くだけで大人(おとな)の犬歯(けんし)の向(む)きが直(なお)ることもあります。3ここでは2つの方法(ほうほう)を3Dモデルで並(なら)べました。矯正(きょうせい)の先生(せんせい)と話(はな)し合(あ)うための資料(しりょう)で、どちらがよいかを決(き)めるものではありません。

When each option is used

Opening up and braces is the usual choice for a young person. It suits someone who will wear braces for two to three years and whose canine is not too far out of place.1 Taking it out is chosen in a few cases. The canine may be very far from its place or fused to the bone. The front teeth may already sit well, so the gap can be closed. The person may not want long treatment. In adults, the chance of success is lower.179

Two other paths exist. Between ages 10 and 13, the dentist can take out the baby canine alone. In one trial, 69 in 100 adult canines then came through by themselves, against 39 in 100 with waiting.3 The canine can also be left in place and checked with X-rays, if it is not harming the teeth next to it. This keeps a small risk of cysts and root damage.1

それぞれの方法(ほうほう)を選(えら)ぶとき

ほかに2つの道(みち)があります。10〜13歳(さい)では、乳歯(にゅうし)の犬歯(けんし)を抜(ぬ)くだけで、大人(おとな)の犬歯(けんし)が自分(じぶん)で生(は)えてくる見込(みこ)みは100本(ぽん)中(ちゅう)69本(ほん)で、待(ま)つだけの39本(ほん)より高(たか)くなりました。3となりの歯(は)に害(がい)がなければ、犬歯(けんし)をそのままにしてレントゲンで見守(みまも)ることもできますが、のう胞(ほう)や根(ね)の傷(きず)の小(ちい)さな危険(きけん)は残(のこ)ります。1

Questions to think about

Good decision aids help you weigh what matters most to you.10 These questions may help before you talk with your braces specialist.

  • Are you, or is your child, ready for two to three years of braces and regular visits?
  • How much does keeping the natural canine matter, compared with a shorter treatment?
  • If the canine came out, what would you want in the gap, and when?
  • How far is the canine from its place, and have the front teeth next to it been checked for root damage?

Evidence

  • FDS RCS England guideline: management options are no treatment with monitoring, interceptive extraction of the deciduous canine, surgical exposure with orthodontic alignment, surgical removal, and autotransplantation; the choice depends on canine position, age, incisor root resorption and patient motivation.1
  • Open versus closed exposure: Cochrane review found no difference in alignment success (RR 0.99), with low-certainty evidence;4 a Swedish multicentre RCT (n=117) aligned all canines in both arms in equal time, with more pain and analgesic use after closed exposure.5 Periodontal outcome: about 0.5 mm more attachment loss on the aligned canine than the contralateral, similar for both techniques.8
  • Treatment duration: 25.8 months (unilateral) and 32.3 months (bilateral) versus 22.4 months in controls.6 In adults, orthodontic resolution succeeded in 69.5% versus 100% in young patients.7
  • Interceptive extraction of the deciduous canine at 10 to 13 years: eruption of the palatally displaced canine in 69% versus 39% with observation (RCT).3 Incisor root resorption adjacent to ectopic canines on CT: 48% of canines.2
  • Indications for removal and prosthetic or orthodontic space management are from the guideline and a narrative review; no RCT compares exposure with removal.19
  • This page follows the IPDAS ideas of presenting options, outcome probabilities with the same denominator and values clarification,10 but it has not been tested or certified as a patient decision aid.
