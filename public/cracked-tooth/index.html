What the models show

Tap Bite down to see what happens when you chew. Tap Cut in half to see inside: the hard outer enamel, the dentine under it, and the pulp (the nerve and blood vessels) in the middle.

The crack is drawn wider than a real crack, and the movement when you bite is made much bigger than real life, so that you can see them. The filling is shown in blue and the crown in grey so they stand out. Real fillings and crowns are usually tooth-coloured.

What is a cracked tooth?

A cracked tooth has a crack that starts on the chewing surface and runs down toward the root. The tooth has not split into pieces, but the crack can slowly spread.1 The American Association of Endodontists (AAE) separates it from craze lines (tiny cracks only in the enamel), a fractured cusp, a split tooth and a vertical root fracture.12 These models show a crack that stays in the crown of the tooth, above the gum.

Cracked teeth do not always hurt. In a large study of 2,858 cracked back teeth in US dental practices, about half (46%) caused pain. The most common pain was to cold, then pain on biting.3

Why a filling may not be enough

A back tooth has pointed parts called cusps. Ridges of tooth along the front and back edges (the marginal ridges) join the cusps together like the walls of a box. A filling that goes across the tooth and through both of these ridges removes the joins. In a laboratory study on extracted teeth, a cavity like this made the cusps on average 63% less stiff.4

A filling sits inside the tooth. When you bite, the cusps can still bend away from each other, and the crack can open and spread. A crown sits over the tooth and wraps around the cusps, so it holds them together and covers the crack.57 Choose Filling, then Crown, and tap Bite down to compare.

What the studies show

StudyWhat it found
Review of 27 studies (2024)6For cracked teeth with a healthy nerve, a filling that did not cover the cusps had about 3 times the risk of nerve problems and about 8 times the risk of the tooth being taken out, compared with a full crown. For root-filled cracked teeth, no crown meant about 11 times the risk of the tooth being taken out.
Singapore, 199 cracked teeth (2019)771% were still healthy after 3 years. Teeth without a full crown were more likely to develop nerve problems: lasting inflammation of the pulp or a dead nerve (odds ratio 8.7).
USA, 127 cracked teeth with mild nerve irritation (2007)8All were planned for crowns. About 1 in 5 later needed root canal treatment, usually within the first months.

A crown gives the most protection but does not guarantee the tooth will be saved.1 Most of this evidence comes from observational studies, not randomised trials, so the numbers are uncertain.611 A crack that causes no symptoms may only need to be watched: in the US study, dentists recommended treatment for about one in three cracked teeth, and the 2024 review found that 80% of cracked teeth that were only watched were still fine after 3 years.36 If the crack reaches the pulp, root canal treatment and a crown may be needed.19 Other restorations that cover the cusps, such as an onlay, are also used.5

When to see your dentist

  • A back tooth that hurts when you bite.
  • A tooth that hurts with cold food or drinks.
  • A line you can see on a tooth.

These were the most common signs in the US study of cracked teeth.3

Your dentist will check the tooth and explain the choices for you, including whether it needs treatment now.

For clinicians: how the models were made

  • Base: the lower right first molar (46) and its pulp from Z-Anatomy, placed in a tooth frame. The CEJ is set 7.5 mm below the highest cusp tip (typical mandibular first molar crown length).10 Enamel thickness is estimated (up to about 1.5 mm on the cusps, none below the CEJ).
  • Crack: mesiodistal, along the central groove, through both marginal ridges. Its deepest point is 2.6 mm above the CEJ in the middle and 0.8 mm above the CEJ at the marginal ridges, so it stays in the crown and clear of the pulp. Drawn 0.24 mm wide; real cracks are far narrower.
  • Filling: MOD composite. Isthmus 3.0 mm wide, pulpal floor 3.6 mm above the CEJ, proximal boxes 4.2 mm wide with gingival floors 1.4 mm above the CEJ.
  • Crown: full coverage, chamfer margin 0.5 mm above the CEJ, 0.7 mm at the margin, 1.2 mm axial and 1.6 mm occlusal reduction. The crack stays in the prepared tooth under the crown.
  • Biting: the buccal and lingual halves of the crown tip apart 6 degrees each about the bottom of the crack (cracked and filled teeth). This is exaggerated many times for teaching; real cusp movement is far too small to see. The crowned and healthy teeth do not move.
  • Cavity, crown and enamel dimensions are typical teaching values, not taken from one source.
