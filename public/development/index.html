Ages and order of eruption for each stage: London Atlas, ADA and AAPD charts.145 Root formation: Moorrees et al.3

Every child is different. The normal age for each tooth can vary by a year or more. So teeth often come through earlier or later than shown here. This is usually normal.45

What the model shows

The model shows the right side of the upper and lower jaw. We cut away the bone on the cheek side, so you can see the adult teeth growing inside the jaw. Baby teeth are blue, adult teeth are yellow, and bone is cream. Tap See-through bone to see all the growing teeth at once.

Choose an age to see what usually happens at that age. The ages are averages from large studies of how teeth grow.12

How an adult tooth grows

Each adult tooth starts as a crown (the top part you see in the mouth) deep in the jaw. The root grows down from the crown. As the root grows, the tooth moves toward the gum.3

An adult front tooth or premolar grows under a baby tooth. As it moves up, the root of the baby tooth melts away. The baby tooth gets loose and falls out. The adult tooth then comes through in its place.6

The adult molars are different. They come through behind the baby teeth and do not replace any baby tooth. The first molars come at about 6 and the second molars at about 12. Wisdom teeth (if they come) arrive in the late teens or later.1

What this looks like on a real X-ray

Dentists see growing teeth on one large X-ray of all the teeth and both jaws. These two are from real people. Their authors released them into the public domain; the licence is not the model licence.

When to ask your dentist

  • A tooth comes through on one side, but its partner on the other side is still missing 6 months later.7
  • An adult tooth comes through but the baby tooth in front of it is still firm after a few months.
  • Your child has pain, swelling, or a tooth that looks dark or broken.

The first point is a sign that teeth are late coming through, from a review of late teeth.7 The other points are general advice to see a dentist, not findings from a specific study. Your dentist can take an X-ray to see the growing teeth, as this model does.

Prototype. Tooth timings are typical ages, checked against the American Dental Association and AAPD eruption charts and the London Atlas.451 The baby teeth are made by resizing adult tooth shapes to typical baby tooth widths and lengths.8 Their crown and root shapes are therefore not true baby tooth shapes. A growing jaw is shown by shrinking the back of an adult jaw to the average child size for each age, from the University of Michigan growth study.1011 The upright back part of the jaw (the ramus) is 76% of adult height in the 6-year model and 89% in the 12-year model; the jaw body is 80% and 94% of adult length (boys and girls averaged). The ramus grows later than the jaw body, with a growth spurt at puberty.9 The missing length is taken from behind the first molar, where the space for the back molars keeps growing through childhood.12 The upper jaw behind the first molar is shortened by the same amount, which is our estimate. This is a simplification: the rest of the jaw keeps its adult shape, and the averages come from 2D X-rays of American children from the 1950s to 1970s. Colours (baby teeth blue, adult teeth yellow) follow the Schour and Massler chart.2 Adult and baby teeth are in their typical places; real teeth vary in position and angle.
