How your child's teeth grow: ages 6 to 12
  • Baby teeth
  • Adult teeth and bone

Every child is different. Teeth often come through a year or more earlier or later than shown here. This is usually normal.

What the model shows

The model shows the right side of the upper and lower jaw. We cut away the bone on the cheek side, so you can see the adult teeth growing inside the jaw. Baby teeth are blue. Adult teeth and bone are cream. Tap See-through bone to see all the growing teeth at once.

Choose an age to see what usually happens at that age. The ages are averages from large studies of how teeth grow.12

How an adult tooth grows

Each adult tooth starts as a crown (the top part you see in the mouth) deep in the jaw. The root grows down from the crown. As the root grows, the tooth moves toward the gum.3

An adult front tooth or premolar grows under a baby tooth. As it moves up, the root of the baby tooth melts away. The baby tooth gets loose and falls out. The adult tooth then comes through in its place.

The adult molars are different. They come through behind the baby teeth and do not replace any baby tooth. The first molars come at about 6 and the second molars at about 12. Wisdom teeth (if they come) arrive in the late teens or later.1

When to ask your dentist

  • A tooth comes through on one side, but its partner on the other side is still missing 6 months later.
  • An adult tooth comes through but the baby tooth in front of it is still firm after a few months.
  • Your child has pain, swelling, or a tooth that looks dark or broken.

Your dentist can take an X-ray to see the growing teeth, as this model does.

About this model

This page is for education. It does not replace advice from your dentist.

Prototype. Tooth timings are typical textbook ages and are being checked against the London Atlas.1 The baby teeth are made by resizing adult tooth shapes to typical baby tooth sizes. A growing jaw is shown by shortening the back of an adult jaw, which is a simplification. Adult and baby teeth are in their typical places; real teeth vary in position and angle.

3D model: made by Vivatheca from Z-Anatomy (the libre 3D atlas of anatomy, CC BY-SA 4.0), which uses BodyParts3D (The Database Center for Life Science, CC BY-SA 2.1 Japan). Changed: teeth and jaw shapes extracted, resized, moved and cut away for each age. This model is licensed under CC BY-SA 4.0.
