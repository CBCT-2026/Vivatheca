Mandibular third molar close to the inferior alveolar nerve: surgical removal or coronectomy下歯槽神経(かしそうしんけい)に近(ちか)い下顎(かがく)第三大臼歯(だいさんだいきゅうし)：抜歯(ばっし)か歯冠(しかん)切除(せつじょ)（コロネクトミー）か

Terms:用語(ようご)： Faculty of Dental Surgery, Royal College of Surgeons of England. Parameters of care for patients undergoing mandibular third molar surgery. 2020.

This page is for a lower wisdom tooth that needs to come out. An X-ray or scan shows its roots close to the nerve. That nerve gives feeling to your lower lip and chin.141

Your surgeon may offer two ways to treat it. In surgical removal, the whole tooth comes out. In coronectomy, only the top of the tooth (the crown) comes out, and the roots stay.1 Here you can see the two side by side, with a 3D model for each. Use them to talk it through with your surgeon. This page does not choose for you.

このページは、抜(ぬ)く必要(ひつよう)がある下(した)の親知(おやし)らずで、レントゲンやCTで根(ね)が神経(しんけい)に近(ちか)い場合(ばあい)のためのものです。この神経(しんけい)は、下(した)くちびるとあごの感覚(かんかく)を伝(つた)えます。141

治療(ちりょう)の方法(ほうほう)は2つあります。1つは歯(は)を全部(ぜんぶ)抜(ぬ)く方法(ほうほう)です。もう1つはコロネクトミーで、歯(は)の頭(あたま)だけを取(と)り、根(ね)は残(のこ)します。1ここでは2つを3Dモデルで並(なら)べました。担当(たんとう)の先生(せんせい)と話(はな)し合(あ)うための資料(しりょう)で、どちらがよいかを決(き)めるものではありません。

Option 1: surgical removal方法(ほうほう)1：歯(は)を全部(ぜんぶ)抜(ぬ)く

The whole tooth comes out.歯(は)を全部(ぜんぶ)取(と)り出(だ)します。

The wisdom tooth is tilted forward against the tooth in front. Its roots lie around the nerve (yellow). Part of the cheek-side bone is cut away in this model so you can see inside.

親知(おやし)らずは前(まえ)の歯(は)にもたれるように傾(かたむ)いています。根(ね)は神経(しんけい)（黄色(きいろ)）を囲(かこ)んでいます。中(なか)が見(み)えるよう、ほお側(がわ)の骨(ほね)の一部(いちぶ)を取(と)り除(のぞ)いて表示(ひょうじ)しています。

Lower left jaw seen from the cheek side, with the wisdom tooth tilted forward against the tooth in front. The yellow nerve runs between its roots. ほお側(がわ)から見(み)た左下(ひだりした)のあご。親知(おやし)らずが前(まえ)の歯(は)にもたれて傾(かたむ)き、黄色(きいろ)の神経(しんけい)が根(ね)のあいだを通(とお)っています。

The surgeon opens the gum and drills away some bone around the crown (orange). Then the tooth is cut into pieces: the crown, and the roots one by one. Each piece comes out on its own, so less force is needed.1011

歯(は)ぐきを開(ひら)き、歯(は)の頭(あたま)のまわりの骨(ほね)（オレンジ）を少(すこ)し削(けず)ります。次(つぎ)に歯(は)を頭(あたま)と根(ね)に分(わ)け、一(ひと)つずつ取(と)り出(だ)します。1011

The same jaw during surgery. A see-through orange shape marks the bone removed around the crown. The crown has been cut off and lifted out, and the two roots are cut apart. 手術(しゅじゅつ)中(ちゅう)の同(おな)じあご。歯(は)の頭(あたま)のまわりの削(けず)る骨(ほね)を透明(とうめい)なオレンジで示(しめ)しています。頭(あたま)は切(き)り離(はな)されて持(も)ち上(あ)がり、2本(ほん)の根(ね)も分(わ)かれています。

All of the tooth is out. Nothing is left near the nerve, so there is nothing to check later. The gum is stitched, and the space fills with bone over the next months.11

歯(は)はすべて取(と)り出(だ)されました。神経(しんけい)の近(ちか)くには何(なに)も残(のこ)りません。歯(は)ぐきを縫(ぬ)い、数(すう)か月(げつ)で穴(あな)は骨(ほね)で埋(う)まります。11

The same jaw after surgery, with no wisdom tooth. The nerve runs alone where the roots were. The orange shape still marks the bone that was removed. 手術(しゅじゅつ)後(ご)の同(おな)じあご。親知(おやし)らずはなく、根(ね)があったところを神経(しんけい)だけが通(とお)っています。削(けず)った骨(ほね)はオレンジのままです。

Option 2: coronectomy方法(ほうほう)2：コロネクトミー（歯冠(しかん)だけを取(と)る）

The crown comes out. The roots stay in the bone.歯(は)の頭(あたま)を取(と)り、根(ね)は骨(ほね)の中(なか)に残(のこ)します。

The same tooth as in option 1. Its roots lie around the nerve (yellow). Part of the cheek-side bone is cut away in this model so you can see inside.

方法(ほうほう)1と同(おな)じ歯(は)です。根(ね)は神経(しんけい)（黄色(きいろ)）を囲(かこ)んでいます。中(なか)が見(み)えるよう、ほお側(がわ)の骨(ほね)の一部(いちぶ)を取(と)り除(のぞ)いて表示(ひょうじ)しています。

Lower left jaw seen from the cheek side, with the wisdom tooth tilted forward against the tooth in front. The yellow nerve runs between its roots. ほお側(がわ)から見(み)た左下(ひだりした)のあご。親知(おやし)らずが前(まえ)の歯(は)にもたれて傾(かたむ)き、黄色(きいろ)の神経(しんけい)が根(ね)のあいだを通(とお)っています。

The surgeon cuts across the tooth just below the crown and lifts the crown out, with all of its enamel. The roots are not moved, so they do not press on the nerve.14

歯(は)の頭(あたま)のすぐ下(した)で歯(は)を切(き)り、エナメル質(しつ)ごと頭(あたま)を取(と)り出(だ)します。根(ね)は動(うご)かさないので、神経(しんけい)を押(お)しません。14

The same jaw during coronectomy. The crown of the wisdom tooth has been cut off and lifted out. The roots stay in place around the yellow nerve. コロネクトミー中(ちゅう)の同(おな)じあご。親知(おやし)らずの頭(あたま)は切(き)り取(と)られて持(も)ち上(あ)がり、根(ね)は黄色(きいろ)の神経(しんけい)のまわりにそのまま残(のこ)っています。

The roots stay in the bone. The surgeon trims their top to at least 3 mm below the top of the bone. Then the gum is closed over them.74

根(ね)は骨(ほね)の中(なか)に残(のこ)ります。根(ね)の上(うえ)の端(はし)は骨(ほね)の上(うえ)の縁(ふち)より3 mm以上(いじょう)低(ひく)く削(けず)り、歯(は)ぐきを縫(ぬ)って閉(と)じます。74

The same jaw after coronectomy. Only the roots of the wisdom tooth are left, low in the bone, around the yellow nerve. The cut top of the roots is shown in pale yellow. コロネクトミー後(ご)の同(おな)じあご。親知(おやし)らずの根(ね)だけが骨(ほね)の低(ひく)いところに残(のこ)り、黄色(きいろ)の神経(しんけい)を囲(かこ)んでいます。根(ね)の切(き)り口(くち)はうすい黄色(きいろ)です。

Most roots move up a little, away from the nerve. In one study they moved 2.8 mm on average over 3 years, mostly in the first year.8 A few roots come up to the gum and need a second, smaller operation.59

多(おお)くの根(ね)は少(すこ)し上(うえ)へ動(うご)き、神経(しんけい)から離(はな)れます。ある研究(けんきゅう)では3年(ねん)で平均(へいきん)2.8 mm動(うご)き、ほとんどは最初(さいしょ)の1年(ねん)でした。8根(ね)が歯(は)ぐきまで出(で)てきて、2回目(かいめ)の小(ちい)さな手術(しゅじゅつ)が要(い)ることもあります。59

The same jaw some years after coronectomy. The roots have moved up a little, so there is a gap between them and the yellow nerve. コロネクトミーの数年後(すうねんご)の同(おな)じあご。根(ね)が少(すこ)し上(うえ)へ動(うご)き、黄色(きいろ)の神経(しんけい)とのあいだにすき間(ま)ができています。

When coronectomy is not used

It is only for a healthy tooth with roots close to the nerve. A guideline lists teeth it is not used for.1 These include a tooth that has died inside or has deep decay. They also include a loose tooth, or one with infection at the root tip.1 It is not used for some cysts and growths either.1

The same guideline asks for care if you have a weak immune system or diabetes.1 The same is true if you have had radiotherapy to the head and neck. It is also true if you cannot easily come back when there is a problem.1

You can also ask about not treating the tooth for now. Your surgeon can tell you the risks of waiting for your own tooth.1

Questions to think about

Good decision aids help you weigh what matters most to you.15 These questions may help before you talk with your surgeon.

  • How much would a numb lip or chin matter to you?
  • Would you accept a small chance of a second operation later, to keep the nerve safer now?
  • If you keep the roots, can you come back for check-ups and X-rays?
  • Is your own tooth right for keeping the roots?

Evidence

  • IAN injury: Leung and Cheung RCT (349 teeth with radiographic signs of proximity), 9/178 (5.1%) after total removal vs 1/155 successful coronectomies (0.6%); 3 of the 9 after removal persisted beyond 12 months.241 Renton et al. RCT: 19% after extraction, 0% after successful and 8% after failed coronectomy.3 Pooled RR 0.1 (95% CI 0.0-0.2; 34 observational studies, 7,115 teeth)5 and RR 0.09 for paraesthesia (RCT meta-analysis).6
  • Lingual nerve RR 0.2 (95% CI 0.0-0.7), dry socket RR 0.4, infection RR 1.0; reintervention 3.63%, root exposure 2.66%, failure 2.79%.5 Failure (root mobilisation, total removal) 9.4% (16/171)2 to 38% (36/94).3
  • Pain at 1 week 57.3% vs 41.9%; dry socket 2.8% vs 0%; infection 6.7% vs 5.8% (Leung and Cheung, as tabulated by Martin et al.).4 Renton et al.: dry socket 9.6% vs 12% (successful coronectomy).3
  • Root migration: mean 2.8 mm (SD 1.4) at 36 months, mostly within 12 months, none after 24 months; 3% root eruption needing removal.8 Over 10 or more years, 15.2% (55/362) reoperation (exposed roots 8.0%, pain 3.9%, infection 1.9%); no nerve deficit after reoperation.9
  • Certainty: GRADE very low to moderate in the RCT meta-analysis.6 The Cochrane review of surgical techniques found no usable trial data on coronectomy for its primary outcomes.13 Across all lower third molar removals, reported IAN injury is 0.35 to 8.4%, and permanent injury is rare.19
  • Selection and contraindications follow the FDS RCS England parameters of care (Appendix 7).1 Radiographic signs of proximity: Rood and Shehab.14
  • This page follows the IPDAS ideas of presenting options, outcome probabilities with the same denominator and values clarification,15 but it has not been tested or certified as a patient decision aid.
