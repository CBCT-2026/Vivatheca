What the model shows

The model shows the right side of the upper jaw, life size. It is cut from front to back through the upper first molar (a back tooth). You look at the cut face. Above the teeth is the maxillary sinus (an air space in the cheekbone). It drains into the nose. The roots of the upper back teeth sit just under the floor of the sinus.67

OAC and OAF are not the same

An oroantral communication (OAC) is a new hole between the mouth and the sinus. It usually forms when a dentist takes out an upper back tooth. An oroantral fistula (OAF) is an OAC that stays open. Over time, skin-like lining (epithelium) grows into the hole, and it becomes a lasting tunnel.1

Who is at risk

  • Roots close to or inside the sinus. The upper first and second molars are closest. In CT scans (3D X-rays), the average gap between root tip and sinus is about 0 to 4 mm at these teeth. Some roots reach into the sinus.678
  • The tooth. Many reports say taking out an upper first molar is the most common cause.19
  • Other factors. Reports also list older age, a root that breaks during removal, and infection at the root tip. Roots that seem to enter the sinus on an X-ray are another sign.110

A panoramic X-ray (one X-ray of all the teeth) works well when there is a clear gap between roots and sinus. When roots seem to touch or enter the sinus, a 3D scan (CBCT) shows this more clearly.11

Signs of a hole

  • Drinks or food come out of your nose on the same side
  • Air passes between your mouth and nose, or you hear a whistle
  • A bad taste, pus, a blocked nose or pain on that side (signs of sinus infection)

A very small hole may cause no signs at all.1 If you notice any of these after an upper tooth comes out, contact your dentist or oral surgeon. (This last point is general advice, not a finding from a study.)

After the hole is closed

Your surgeon will usually ask you to:1

  • not blow your nose
  • sneeze with your mouth open
  • avoid hard exercise for a while
  • eat soft food.

You may also get pain relief and medicine to unblock your nose.1 Follow your own surgeon's advice.

For clinicians: decision points from the literature{医療者向|いりょうしゃむ}け：{文献|ぶんけん}にもとづく{判断|はんだん}のポイント
  • OAC under 2 mm, not epithelialised, no sinus infection: may heal spontaneously after clot formation. Defects over 5 mm, or present for more than 3 weeks, rarely heal spontaneously.12
  • Close an OAC within 24 to 48 hours; persistence increases the chance of maxillary sinusitis.1
  • OAF: excise the epithelial tract, debride to well-perfused tissue and curette infected bone; close in a disease-free sinus, with irrigation and antibiotics first if infected.1
  • Flap margins should rest on intact bone at least 5 mm from the defect, without tension.1
  • Buccal advancement (Rehrmann) flap: small to moderate defects; reduces vestibular depth, which may affect later prostheses. Palatal rotation flap suits premolar sites; buccal fat pad suits posterior sites and can be combined with a buccal flap.1
  • A network meta-analysis of 9 RCTs favoured the buccal fat pad over buccal advancement and palatal rotation flaps for closure and recurrence.4 A 2025 systematic review reports conservative management for defects under 5 mm and surgical closure above 5 mm.3

Prototype, version 0.1. How the model was made: the bone and the two molars come from the Z-Anatomy skull. That maxilla has only a small sinus space, so the sinus was carved inside it, keeping a 1.5 mm wall. Sinus floor heights were set by hand to match published ranges: 3 mm of bone over the root tips in the normal stage, and root tips 4 mm inside the sinus (covered by 0.5 mm of bone) in the other stages.678 The gum is drawn as a 2.25 mm layer on the mouth side (an estimate, not from a source). The opening, the fistula (3.2 mm across), the clot, the flap and the fat pad are drawn to show the idea, not measured from patients. The sinus lining is shown only where it is swollen. Real sizes, shapes and techniques vary. Sources also differ on which openings can be left to heal: Parvini and colleagues give under 2 mm, while a 2025 review reports conservative care for openings under 5 mm.13
