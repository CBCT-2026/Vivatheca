    What is a steel crown?

    A steel crown is a ready-made metal cap. It covers the whole of a baby back tooth. Dentists use one when:14

    • decay has made holes on more than one side of the tooth
    • the dentist has treated the nerve inside the tooth
    • the tooth is broken, or did not grow in the right way
    • your child is likely to get more decay

    Most often, the dentist numbs the tooth and takes out the decay. Then they trim a little off the tooth so the crown fits, and glue the crown on.2 The crown comes out with the baby tooth when the adult tooth comes through.

    Why a crown and not a large filling?

    A filling only replaces the part of the tooth that the decay took away. When the holes are large, the walls of tooth left around the filling are thin. A crown covers the whole tooth. It holds the tooth together, and it leaves no edges where new decay can start.14

    Studies that compared crowns with fillings in baby back teeth found:

    • Crowns were less likely to fail badly, for example with an infection or the tooth needing to come out. They were also less likely to cause pain later.2
    • After 2 years, about 93 in 100 crowns were still working well. For large fillings, it was 80 in 100.6
    • In a 5-year study, 3 in 100 crowns failed badly. For fillings, it was about 17 in 100.3

    Dentists for children in the USA advise steel crowns for some children. These are children who are likely to get more decay and have large holes in their baby back teeth.1 For a small hole, a filling can still be the right choice.1 Ask your child's dentist which is best for your child.

    The Hall Technique

    Some dentists use another way, called the Hall Technique. The dentist glues a steel crown over the tooth as it is. They do not numb the tooth, take out the decay or trim the tooth. The crown seals the decay in, which helps stop it.13

    The Shaped stage in the model shows the usual way. With the Hall Technique, the crown would go over the tooth as it is.
