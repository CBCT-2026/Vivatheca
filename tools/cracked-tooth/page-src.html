What the model shows

These models show one back tooth from the lower jaw. You can see it healthy, cracked, with a filling and with a crown. Tap Bite down to see what happens when you chew. Tap Cut in half to see inside the tooth.

The hard outer layer is the enamel. Under it is the dentine (the main body of the tooth). In the middle is the pulp (the nerve and blood vessels).

We drew the crack wider than a real one. We also made the bending much bigger than in real life, so you can see it. The filling is blue and the crown is grey so they stand out. Real ones are most often the colour of your teeth.

What is a cracked tooth?

A cracked tooth has a crack that starts on the top of the tooth, where you chew. It runs down toward the root. The tooth has not split into pieces. But the crack can slowly grow.1

This is not the same as a split tooth, a broken-off cusp or a crack in the root. Tiny lines in the outer layer only (craze lines) are not the same either.12 This model shows a crack that stays in the top part of the tooth, above the gum.

A cracked tooth does not always hurt. In a large US study of 2,858 cracked back teeth, about 46 in 100 caused pain. Most often it hurt with cold. The next most common was pain when biting.3

Why a filling may not be enough

A back tooth has bumps on top called cusps. Ridges along the front and back edges join the cusps, like the walls of a box. A large filling goes right across the tooth and through both ridges. Then the cusps are no longer joined.

In a lab test on pulled teeth, this kind of filling made the cusps much less stiff. On average they lost about 63 in 100 parts of their stiffness.4

A filling sits inside the tooth. When you bite, the cusps can still bend apart. The crack can open and grow.

A crown sits over the tooth. It wraps around all the cusps and holds them together. It also covers the crack.57 Choose Filling, then Crown, and tap Bite down to compare.

What the studies show

Studies found that cracked teeth with a crown had fewer nerve problems than teeth with a filling. Dentists also had to take them out less often.67

A crown gives the most protection. But it cannot promise to save the tooth.1

Most of these studies watched what happened to people over time. They were not trials that chose the treatment by chance. So the numbers are not certain.611

Your dentist may only need to watch a crack that does not hurt. In the US study, dentists advised treatment for about 36 in 100 cracked teeth. In one review, 80 in 100 cracked teeth that dentists only watched were still fine after 3 years.36

If the crack reaches the pulp, you may need root canal treatment and a crown.19 Some dentists use an onlay instead. This is a filling made outside the mouth that covers the top of the tooth.5

When to see your dentist

See your dentist if you have:

  • a back tooth that hurts when you bite
  • a tooth that hurts with cold food or drinks
  • a line you can see on a tooth.

These were the signs people noticed most in the US study.3 Your dentist will check the tooth. They will explain your choices, and if the tooth needs treatment now.
